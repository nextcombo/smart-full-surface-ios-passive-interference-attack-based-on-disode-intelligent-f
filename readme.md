# Smart Full Surface IOS - Passive Interference Attack Based on DISODE Intelligent Full-Surface Surface

With the advent of emerging intelligent omnidirectional surfaces (IOS), which possess 360-degree electromagnetic radiation capabilities, wireless system performance has been significantly enhanced. However, the adversarial IOS poses a significant risk to physical layer security. Here, we propose a full-passive jammer (FPJ) based on the "DISCO" IOS (DIOS), which can initiate an all-directional all-passive jamming attack. The relevant refraction and reflection (R&R) coefficients for the adversarial IOS are randomly generated, serving as akin to a "Disco Ball." By introducing active channel aging (ACA) within the coherence time of the channel, the DIOS-based FPJ can perform all-directional all-passive jamming without relying on the interference power or channel knowledge of legitimate users. To describe the impact of the DIOS-based FPJ, we derive the statistical characteristics of the DIOS-induced jamming channel based on two widely used IOS models (i.e., amplitude modulation and phase modulation). Numerical results indicate that the proposed DIOS-based FPJ can not only perform all-directional all-passive jamming but also enhance the interference impact by 58.8% under a transmit power of 10dBm per legitimate user.
Introduction: Due to the broadcast and superposition characteristics of wireless channels, wireless communication is easily susceptible to malicious attacks such as physical layer interference (a type of DoS attack). In wireless networks, active jammers (AJ) can easily launch jamming attacks, where AJ causes intentional interference, thereby blocking communication between the access point (AP) and legitimate users (LU). Since AJ inherently has a disadvantage in energy, one of the main focuses of AJ research is to develop strategies to maximize the duration and range of effective interference while minimizing the required interference power. However, these AJ require some interference energy consumption to effectively attack LU.
Recently, Reconfigurable Intelligent Surfaces (RIS) have attracted increasing attention as a promising candidate technology for the future 6th Generation (6G) wireless communication. Existing research on RIS focuses mainly on improving performance through the introduction of legitimate RIS and designing appropriate RIS coefficients (e.g., energy efficiency and spectral efficiency). Following RIS, Intelligent Full-Directional Surfaces (IOS) are being introduced into the field of wireless communications to achieve 360-degree performance enhancement. However, the significant adverse effects of adversarial RIS/IOS on wireless communication are increasingly recognized. For example, there are reports in the literature that passive jammers (PJ) based on adversarial RIS can combine reflected path signals with direct path signals to minimize received power. Although this PJ does not consume interference power, adversarial RIS must know all the channel state information (CSI) of the wireless channels. Since RIS is passive, it is unrealistic to assume that an illegal RIS knows CSI.
To address the difficulty of adversarial RIS obtaining CSI, full-passive jammers (FPJ) have been proposed, which can initiate jamming attacks without relying on interference power or CSI. In the FPJ, the role of the adversarial RIS with random reflection coefficients is like a "Disco Ball," hence it is referred to as a "DISCO" RIS (DRIS). The use of DRIS introduces active channel aging (ACA), thereby achieving full-passive jamming. Please note that ACA is different from traditional channel aging (CA), where traditional channel aging is caused by the time variation of radio frequency propagation and the computation delay between the AP learning wireless channels and the precoding time. Although the FPJ based on DRIS can initiate full-passive jamming attacks, there are blind spots in the jamming. That is, the transmissive side LU of the DRIS is completely unaffected by the FPJ based on DRIS.
In order to implement 360-degree interference in a Multi-User, Multi-Input, Single-Output (MU-MISO) system, we investigated full-passive jamming attacks based on adversarial IOS. The main contributions are summarized as follows:
We considered two widely used IOS models, namely the constant amplitude assumption IOS model and the variable amplitude assumption IOS model. Specifically, in these two IOS models, the phase shifts of the IOS elements' transmission and reflection (R&R) are discrete and correlated. Moreover, in the variable amplitude IOS model, the amplitude is a function of its corresponding phase shift.
We proposed an FPJ based on DIOS to perform full-passive jamming attacks. During each coherent time interval composed of the pilot transmission (PT) and data transmission (DT) stages, we randomly generate time-varying R&R coefficients that approximate the length of the PT stage. This introduces ACA to achieve full-passive jamming. Additionally, the proposed FPJ based on DIOS can not only simply superimpose the R&R interference effects but also produce effects that are not merely additive.
To describe the impact of the FPJ based on DIOS, statistical characteristics of the DIOS interference channel were derived for both types of DIOS models. Based on theoretical derivations, some characteristics of the FPJ based on DIOS were given. For example, when using the constant amplitude DIOS, the interference effect does not depend on the quantization of the R&R phase shift or its distribution. However, when using the variable amplitude DIOS, the interference effect depends on both quantization and distribution.
